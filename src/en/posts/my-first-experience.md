I remember reading a passage that talked about generosity, not just with money, but with time and attention. It seemed counter-intuitive in a busy world – give away what you feel you lack?

So, I decided to try a small experiment. For one week, I consciously focused on being more generous with my attention towards colleagues, even when I felt overwhelmed.

## The Experiment

The principle wasn't complex: "Give, and it will be given to you." (Luke 6:38, though the context is broader than just material things). I wasn't expecting a windfall, but maybe... less stress? Better interactions?

1.  **Listen Fully:** When someone spoke to me, put down the phone, stop typing, make eye contact.
2.  **Offer Help Proactively:** If someone looked stuck, ask if they needed a hand, even for 5 minutes.
3.  **Share Credit:** Make sure to mention others' contributions in meetings.

It felt awkward at first.

## Observations

Surprisingly, by the end of the week, I *felt* less stressed, not more. Conversations felt more meaningful. A couple of colleagues spontaneously offered help on one of *my* tasks later that week. Coincidence? Maybe. But the shift in my own feeling of connection and reduced pressure was undeniable.

<details class="callout">
  <summary>Deeper Thought: Why Might This Work?</summary>
  <p>Perhaps focusing outward reduces self-preoccupation, which is often a source of stress. Maybe demonstrating value to others subconsciously encourages reciprocity. Or perhaps, as the source text suggests, there's a principle at play that operates regardless of our full understanding – a sort of 'spiritual physics'.</p>
</details>

This wasn't about a magical formula, but about testing a principle's effect on my own state and relationships. It suggests that some ancient wisdom holds practical, observable value even today.
